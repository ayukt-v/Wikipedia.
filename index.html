Peafowl

“Peacock” redirects here. For other uses, see Peacock (disambiguation).

Peafowl is a common name for three species of large birds. Male peafowl are called peacocks, while females are called peahens. In everyday speech, “peacock” is often used for both sexes.

An Indian peacock displaying his train.

The two Asian species are the Indian peafowl and the green peafowl. The Congo peafowl is native to the Congo Basin in Central Africa. Male peafowl are known for their bright plumage, long feathers and loud calls.

1. Description

Indian peafowl have iridescent blue and green plumage. The male's long display train is made of elongated upper-tail covert feathers, many of which have eye-shaped markings. A crest sits on the head of both males and females.

Peahens usually have brown, grey and green feathers that help them blend into their surroundings. Green peafowl have green and bronze colouring, while Congo peafowl look different from the Asian species.

1.1 Iridescence and structural coloration

The bright colours of peacock feathers are partly produced by tiny structures in the feathers rather than by pigments alone. These structures reflect light in different ways, making the colours appear to change with the viewing angle. This effect is called iridescence.

1.2 Courtship

During courtship, a male Indian peafowl raises and spreads its train into a large fan. It may vibrate the feathers, move around the female and make loud calls. The display gives the peahen an opportunity to observe the male's feathers and behaviour.

2. Evolutionary history

Peafowl belong to the tribe Pavonini, within the pheasant family. Fossils and comparisons with related birds help scientists study how these birds evolved and spread across different regions.

2.1 Sexual selection for plumage

Charles Darwin discussed peacock feathers while developing ideas about sexual selection. Scientists continue to study how feather patterns, displays, calls and other traits influence courtship and survival.

3. Behaviour

Peafowl spend much of their time on the ground searching for food, but often roost in trees. They use calls to communicate and may defend small areas from other birds. Their behaviour can vary between species and habitats.

4. Diet

Peafowl are omnivores. Their diet may include seeds, fruits, flower parts, insects and other small animals. They commonly search through leaf litter and vegetation for food.

5. Cultural significance

The Indian peafowl has a long history in art, stories and religious traditions. It is also the national bird of India and is widely recognised for its colourful appearance.

6. Breeding and colour variations

Peafowl build shallow nests on the ground. The female generally incubates the eggs and cares for the young chicks. Captive populations may also show colour variations that differ from the usual wild plumage.
